Executive summary

14.2% of customers have churned (426/3,000), representing 41.2K in Monthly Recurring Revenue (MRR). Churn is highest in the first 6 months: customers with account age ≤ 6 months churned at 35.2%, 4× the rate of customers > 6 months (8.8%). Monthly plans churned at 17.6%, annual plans at 6.6%. Enterprise: 25 customers, yet 50.2% of lost MRR. The model estimates approximately ~32K MRR from active customers is at similar risk without intervention. 30-day priorities: 90-day onboarding, dunning for payment failures, and a review of 20 Enterprise accounts.

01Signal · Data quality"How should the 14.2% figure be read?"

The Q4 2025 signup cohort has the highest churn: 39.2%, vs 4.5% for the Q1 2023 cohort

Churn rate by signup quarter
Bars: % of cohort churned · small figures: median account age (months)
Source: analysis/descriptive.json → cohort. Monthly signups range from 53 to 122, with no sign of truncated data.

The more recent the signup cohort, the higher the share already churned. 2023 cohorts sit at just 2.4–7.5%, while the Q4 2025 cohort reaches 39.2% despite only 0–3 months of tenure. The chart groups customers by signup date, not churn date.

The dataset is a snapshot with no churn_date. 406/426 churned customers have tenure close to their account age (median gap 0.37 months), so tenure is read as account age.

Implication: 14.2% is the share of customers churned within the snapshot, not a monthly churn rate. The "new customers churn more" effect cannot be separated from the "newer cohorts perform worse" effect.

02Churn rate (customers)"Who is leaving, and why?"

Customers aged 0–3 months churn at 39.2%, 8.7× the rate of long-tenured customers: churn is concentrated in onboarding

Churn rate by account age
% of customers churned in each account-age band (months)
Source: analysis/descriptive.json → seg.tenure_band. χ² = 322, p ≈ 10-68.
Findings

Customers ≤ 6 months churned at 35.2% (215/610), 4× the rate of customers > 6 months (8.8%, 211/2,390).

After 12 months, churn falls to 8.7% (13–24 months) and 4.5% (25–36 months).

Monthly plans churned at 17.6%, annual plans at 6.6%. Within each plan, the gap is 1.7–3.9×. Basic·Monthly is the highest (20.6%).

Churn rate by plan × billing cycle
Monthly exceeds Annual across all three plans
Source: analysis/descriptive.json → plan_billing. χ² Monthly vs Annual within each plan: Basic p ≈ 10-5, Enterprise p < 0.001, Pro p = 0.03.
Validated causes

Plan and billing cycle are two independent drivers. In the multivariate model, Basic has 3.15× the churn odds of Enterprise, and Monthly 2.24× those of Annual (both p < 0.001).

High churn among new customers is not a mix effect: the shares of Basic (52–57%) and Monthly (66–73%) are nearly constant across account-age bands.

No login for more than 14 days → churn rate of 29–49%, vs 8.1% for customers who logged in within 14 days. Yet customers aged 0–3 months whose last login was within 7 days still churn at 36% (table below).

Churn reasons are similar for new and established customers: Too expensive 22.8% vs 23.7%, No longer needed 21.9% vs 19.4% (≤ 6 months vs > 6 months).

Requires internal validation: feature-level usage event logs, onboarding completion rates, and pricing and discount history.

03Lost MRR"Where is revenue being lost, and why does it differ from customer counts?"

25 Enterprise customers account for 50.2% of lost MRR, while Basic accounts for 73.2% of churned customers

Share of churned customers vs share of lost MRR, by plan
% of all 426 churned customers and of total 41.2K lost MRR
Source: analysis/descriptive.json → seg.plan. Enterprise accounts for 71.4% of total MRR.
Findings

The lost-MRR rate (7.5%) is below the customer churn rate (14.2%) because churned customers have an ARPU of 97.6 vs 198.4 for active customers.

Enterprise is the exception: 25 customers, yet 50.2% of lost MRR. Enterprise·Monthly alone: 15 customers account for 28.3%.

Each churned Enterprise customer represents on average ~828 MRR lost, ~24× that of a Basic customer (~34).

Churn reasons: share of customers vs share of lost MRR
The ranking reverses when measured by MRR
Source: analysis/descriptive.json → pareto_reasons. Reason distribution differs across plans (p ≈ 10-10).
Validated causes

By MRR, Switched to competitor (25.3%) and Poor support (16.3%) lead. These two reasons cover 79/426 customers (19%) but 17.1K/41.2K of lost MRR (42%). In Enterprise, 16/25 customers left for these two reasons.

Payment failed accounts for 16.2% of customers and 12.6% of lost MRR. These customers last logged in a median 3 days before leaving, vs 30 days for other reasons: they were still using the product, making this involuntary churn.

Limitation: Enterprise has only n = 25 churned customers (< 30), so support and competitor reasons in Enterprise remain hypotheses. Support tickets, NPS and win/loss data are needed.

04MRR at risk · Scenarios"What happens if nothing changes, and what should we do first?"

An estimated ~32K MRR (6.3% of active MRR) is at risk without intervention, 55.2% of it in Enterprise

Expected lost MRR in the active base, by plan
Sum of churn probability × MRR across 2,574 active customers
Source: analysis/predictive.json. Logistic regression, 5-fold cross-validated AUC = 0.83. The top 20% of customers by risk score capture 60.1% of churned customers.

Applying the model to 2,574 active customers, the sum of (churn probability × MRR) is ~32K, equivalent to about 281 customers (10.9%) who may churn if current conditions persist. The estimate has no time horizon.

By customer count, risk sits in Basic (194 customers). By MRR, risk sits in Enterprise (17.7K). The 20 highest-risk Enterprise accounts represent 31.1% of Enterprise expected lost MRR.

High-risk tier (probability > 30%): 234 customers, 13.8K MRR. This is the priority outreach list for the Customer Success team.

Estimated on the active base · scenarios overlap and are not additive
Source: analysis/predictive.json → scenarios. Assumptions for each scenario are listed in the panel on the right. Results are correlational and require A/B testing.

90-day onboarding: Reduce churn probability by 25% for customers with account age ≤ 6 months → retains about 25 customers, 3.5K MRR.

Dunning (Payment failed): Retain 50% of customers who would churn due to payment failure (share: 16.2% of churned customers, 12.6% of lost MRR) → retains about 23 customers, 2.0K MRR.

Monthly → Annual switch: 20% of active Monthly customers switch to Annual → retains about 21 customers, 1.9K MRR.

Re-engagement: 30% of customers with no login for > 14 days return (login ≤ 7 days) → retains about 13 customers, 1.5K MRR.

★Conclusions & Recommendations

Three P1 initiatives to deliver within 30 days: onboarding and dunning retain an estimated ~5.5K MRR, and an Enterprise review protects 5.5K MRR at risk

1
Churn is concentrated in the first 6 months, and login is not a warning signal at this stage
39.2% of 0–3 month customers churned · customers ≤ 6 months churned at 35.2% · customers with a login in the last 0–7 days still churned at 36%  (§02)
Recommendation

Roll out a 90-day onboarding playbook for new customers (check-ins on days 7/30/60), measured by "value achieved" milestones rather than login counts. For price-sensitive customers, offer a downgrade instead of cancel.

A 25% risk reduction across 395 customers ≤ 6 months equates to retaining about 25 customers, 3.5K MRR.

High priority

Enable dunning: automatic payment retries, plus email and in-app reminders to update card details before expiry.

Retaining 50% of this group equates to about 23 customers, 2.0K MRR.

A/B test a Monthly-to-Annual conversion offer for the 1,703 active Monthly customers.

Appendix · Reliability notes and data limitations. Unanswered causal layer: why new customers find the product not worth the cost, or no longer need it, within the first 3 months. The dataset lacks feature-level usage, onboarding completion, pricing/discount and acquisition-channel data needed to answer this. (1) No churn_date: 14.2% is a snapshot rate, not a monthly churn rate. If the export omits earlier churned customers, this figure is understated. (2) The risk model is trained on the same snapshot. ~32K MRR at risk is an estimate with no time horizon. Scenarios are correlational; actual results may be lower than estimated if self-selection is present (e.g., more loyal customers self-selecting into Annual). (3) Enterprise has n = 25 churned customers; conclusions on Enterprise churn reasons are hypotheses. (4) 15 negative-revenue rows were excluded from MRR, so total MRR is understated if these are sign errors. If the 17 Enterprise rows at 2,000 are capped, Enterprise MRR is also understated.
